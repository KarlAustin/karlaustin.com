Another year, another renewal notice from WebPros, and another round of eye-watering price increases. If you run a hosting business, you know the drill by now. Every autumn, we sit down with a spreadsheet, squint at the new licensing tiers for cPanel and Plesk, and try to work out how much of that cost we can absorb before we're forced to pass it on to customers.

Give them their dues: for years, both panels were the undisputed backbone of shared hosting. They worked, clients knew how to use them, and the licensing was predictable enough that you didn't lose sleep over it. That era is well and truly dead.


## Death by a Thousand Upsells

The price hikes would be easier to swallow if the core product was getting dramatically better for hosting providers. Instead, we're paying more every year to maintain stacks that feel increasingly long in the tooth. Underneath the fresh coat of UI paint sits decades of legacy architecture that still requires plenty of babysitting.

Rather than fixing the fundamentals or making life easier for sysadmins, WebPros seems obsessed with bolting on fluff features nobody asked for.  It's not always the big things either, it's the everyday little things, the lack of mobile views for admins, the fact the 2FA code box doesn't trigger a number-pad on mobile etc. there's 100s of them.

Worse still though, whilst they are neglecting us admins, they're turning our own control panels into a shopfront for their own add-ons.

* **Aggressive upselling** - using hosting providers as a free sales channel to push WebPros-owned extras directly to our end users.

* **Forced WebPros accounts** - requiring end users to sign up for a WebPros account just to use basic new features, needlessly muddying the support boundary between us and our clients.

* **Invasive tracking** - telemetry and tracking baked into the panels that nobody in a privacy-conscious UK or European hosting outfit wants anywhere near their stack.

When a vendor starts treating your customer base as their own direct sales funnel, the relationship has fundamentally broken down. You're paying them for the privilege of letting them poach margin from your own clients.


## So, What's the Fix?

For a long time, migrating away from cPanel or Plesk was a non-starter. The engineering overhead of moving thousands of sites and retraining clients who just want to check their webmail made swallowing the licensing fee the lesser of two evils.

That maths doesn't work anymore. The gap between the cost of a WebPros licence and the effort required to migrate has finally flipped, and the market has woken up to it.

* **Enhance** - built from the ground up with modern containerisation and multi-server clustering in mind, avoiding the single-server legacy baggage of cPanel.

* **DirectAdmin** - the ever-reliable workhorse that has quietly kept its head down, stayed fast, and avoided taking the piss on pricing.

* **The new wave** - a steady stream of newer panels springing up almost monthly, driven by developers who can smell blood in the water.

* **Rolling your own** - for teams with the engineering headcount, building a lightweight internal orchestration layer on top of standard Linux tooling is looking far more attractive than it did five years ago.

None of these options are a zero-effort drop-in replacement. Migrating legacy estates is still a faff, and you have to weigh the maintenance burden against your team's actual capacity. At least you're investing engineering time into an asset you control, rather than funding a vendor's next round of price rises.


## The Venture Capital Playbook

None of this is accidental, and none of it should surprise anyone who has watched private equity and venture capital tear through the infrastructure space over the last decade.

WebPros isn't trying to build the best hosting control panel for the next twenty years. They are doing exactly what venture capital always does: taking solid, sticky, profitable software businesses and squeezing every last penny out of them while the inertia still holds. They're betting that migrating is too painful, so we'll all keep paying.


## You know where this is going...

We've been a WebPros partner for 25 years, and walking away from a stack that long isn't something we take lightly. Even so, we're actively looking at ditching them. When a vendor stops acting like a partner and starts squeezing our margins at every turn, loyalty only goes so far.
