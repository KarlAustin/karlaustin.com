I've been a Vodafone broadband customer for years. Like a lot of people working from home in tech, i've traditionally run a business account on my residential line, mostly because it's the easiest way to get a static IP and, in theory at least, slightly better support when things go sideways.

My current contract term recently expired. Since they already hit me with the usual annual price rise every April anyway, coming out of the minimum term was mostly just my prompt to sit down, look at what renewal options were available, and see if I could get a better deal, or whether it was time to jump ship. How hard can it be?

## The self-service dead end

As a business customer, Vodafone shows renewal plans in the portal with a button to click through, only to tell me I can't actually upgrade online. It's a pure tease, just a prompt to ring them up or use live chat.

I went for the live chat on my desktop, only for the widget to refuse to load entirely. A quick look under the hood showed it was getting caught in my privacy filters because of all the third-party trackers bundled into the chat script. Fair enough, I'll use the mobile app instead.

The chat widget actually loaded in the app, which felt like a small victory until the chatbot kicked in. I got trapped in an endless loop of canned questions that completely missed what I was trying to ask. Every attempt to nudge it towards an actual human just reset the script.

## Jumping ship to EE (or trying to)

At that point, I gave up on renewing and decided it might be much easier to switch providers entirely. EE had an offer on for a 1.6 Gbit/s line, a solid bump over my existing 910 Mbit/s, for a 1/3 less money and with better WiFi kit thrown in to boot.

I started the online order process, expecting the One Touch Switch system to do the heavy lifting. Instead, EE's checkout choked and threw an error the moment I fed it my Vodafone broadband account details.

Right on cue, up popped the exact same message: *"Call us or use live chat."*

## The forty-minute reset

To give EE its dues, the live chat offered to put me in a queue for a human within about 30 seconds, promising a five-minute wait.

Forty minutes later, an agent finally picked up.

They asked which specific package I was looking at, since there's the headline 1.6 Gbit/s speed and then a handful of different add-on tiers. I opened a new tab to grab the exact package name off their site so I didn't give them the wrong one. When I switched back, the chat session looked like it had reset itself.

Another forty minutes in the queue? No chance. Thankfully, logging back into my new EE account brought the session back and the chat was still going, saving me from starting from scratch.

## Why is it this hard?

I work in tech and I find it a pain.

We spend around £2,500 a year with Vodafone in total, and we influence roughly the same again elsewhere. So they aren't just losing a £60 (+ VAT) a month broadband deal, they're potentially losing a lot more than that going forwards.

Between fake-out portal buttons, trackers breaking support tools, bots acting as bouncers, and fragile web sessions, the whole experience is broken. It really shouldn't be this hard.

### Update

We eventually got there, and someone from Openreach will be turning up to replace my "modem" on 9 December.  Why I can't just unplug the PSU, fibre and Cat6 myself and plug a new ONT is anyone's guess.
